Objectives
 * Understand the nomenclature, calculations, and design parameters associated with helical springs.
 * Generate precise 3D CAD models of one compression spring and two extension springs using industrial specifications.
 * Perform Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on the compression spring to evaluate stress concentrations and validate the design.
 * Observe and document the real-world sequence of spring manufacturing on the factory floor.
Software and Tools Utilized
 * CAD Modeling: SOLIDWORKS was utilized to convert 2D specifications into 3D models.
 * Finite Element Analysis: ANSYS Workbench (Static Structural) was used to simulate loads and evaluate structural integrity.
Key Technical Phases
1. CAD Modeling
 * Compression Spring: Modeled with closed and ground ends to ensure a flat seating surface, which prevents buckling under axial loads.
 * Extension Spring 1: Designed with standard "Machine Half Hooks" by utilizing 3D sketches to create the transitional bend.
 * Extension Spring 2: Designed for heavier applications featuring "Crossover Loops," where the wire crosses the center of the spring body.
2. Finite Element Analysis (FEA)
 * The simulation utilized AISI 1020 Steel (Cold Drawn), which has a defined tensile yield strength of 350 MPa.
 * An axial compressive force of 100 N was applied to the top ground face of the spring, while the bottom face was fixed.
 * The maximum Equivalent (von-Mises) stress observed was 332.07 MPa, located heavily along the inner curvature of the active coils due to the Wahl curvature effect.
 * Because the peak stress (332.07 MPa) remained below the material's yield strength (350 MPa), the design was confirmed to be structurally safe and within its elastic limits.
3. Manufacturing Processes
 * Winding and Coiling: CNC machines shape the wire into a helical form using deflection and pitch tools.
 * Heat Treatment (Stress Relieving): Coiled springs are baked in conveyor ovens to relieve residual bending stresses induced during winding.
 * End Grinding: Compression springs are passed between parallel grinding wheels to create perfectly flat, load-bearing ends.
 * Shot Peening: Spherical steel media bombards the springs to induce compressive residual stress, effectively improving the component's fatigue life.
 * Setting/Presetting: Springs are compressed to their solid height to induce minor plastic deformation, which permanently stabilizes their length for future use.
 * Surface Finishing: Final treatments like Zinc Electroplating, Black Oxide coating, or Powder Coating are applied for corrosion resistance and aesthetic purposes.
The internship successfully bridged academic theory and industrial practice by integrating virtual CAD design and FEA simulation with the physical constraints of Design for Manufacturability (DFM). Analyzing the entire lifecycle of a spring reinforced the necessity of factoring physical shop-floor processes, such as residual stress relieving and end grinding, directly into initial theoretical models.
